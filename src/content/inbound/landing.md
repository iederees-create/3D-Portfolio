## The question that rarely makes the project plan

Most S/4HANA programmes have a clear plan for data, custom code and testing. Business documents are often handled later, or not at all. A useful way to test your position is one question:

> **If you switched your existing SAP archive off tomorrow, what would you do with all the historical documents that still need to be retained and accessed?**

If the answer is not obvious, you are not alone. The starting point is different for every organisation, and it usually looks like one of the three situations below.

---

<!-- THREE SITUATIONS -->

## Which situation sounds like yours?

### 1. You are moving from ECC to S/4HANA

You are planning or running a migration to S/4HANA, on SAP Private Cloud or on-premise. The open questions are about the past, not the future:

- Are you migrating every historical document, keeping the legacy system running, or decommissioning it?
- How will users reach documents linked to ECC transactions once that system is retired?
- Is the archive part of the transformation plan, or an afterthought?

**What a good outcome looks like:** archive strategy is decided as part of the S/4HANA programme, so historical documents stay retained and accessible without keeping every legacy application alive.

### 2. You have no structured document management system

Documents related to SAP transactions live on shared network drives, in email attachments or on paper. People know where to look, until they leave or the drive is reorganised.

- How do your people find a document related to an SAP transaction today, and how long does that usually take?
- Can you show what was kept, and when, if an auditor or customer asks?

**What a good outcome looks like:** documents are linked to the SAP transactions they belong to, stored under a clear retention policy, and easy to find.

### 3. You use SharePoint, but it is not properly integrated with SAP

SharePoint is a capable platform, and many manufacturers already use it. The question is whether it is actually connected to SAP.

- Is someone manually uploading and matching documents to SAP records by hand?
- Do SAP users have to leave the transaction to find the file they need?
- Who owns retention rules for SAP-related content stored in SharePoint?

**What a good outcome looks like:** SAP-related documents are linked to SAP automatically, governed consistently, and reachable from where people work.

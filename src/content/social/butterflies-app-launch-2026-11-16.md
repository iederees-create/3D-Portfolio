## LinkedIn — launch post

A question from a school project turned into my latest release: could parents keep the school on their phone, with the same account they already use on the website?

I’ve now built an installable web app for Butterflies Educare in Cape Town.

The work includes:
• One account across the website and app
• Family profiles, class requests and event participation
• Enrolment summaries and an account-linking tracker for the school team
• A request process for people who want to offer classes
• 15 fundraising ideas with income, costs, surplus scenarios and break-even calculations

One detail I particularly enjoyed: the principal can simply choose a fundraising idea. That choice stays a draft while we work through costs, bookings and approval.

Stack: JavaScript, HTML/CSS, Supabase Auth, PostgreSQL and GitHub Pages. The app installs through the browser; it isn’t an app-store listing.

The build and live deployment checks passed. Device testing and school rollout are the next steps, so I’m sharing what I’ve built rather than claiming adoption or fundraising results already.

## Follow-up post — engineering decision

Why did I build the school app on the same system as the website?

Because a parent shouldn’t need a second account just to open the school from their phone’s home screen.

For Butterflies Educare, I added a progressive web app around the existing school pages and backend. Same email and password, same records, same permissions.

I also kept private profiles out of the service worker cache. When a page can’t load offline, the app explains that an internet connection is needed.

A small product decision can remove a lot of friction.

## Follow-up post — financial feature

“How will this event actually raise money?” was a useful product question.

For a school project, I turned that question into a comparison tool with 15 fundraising ideas. Each has an income model, estimated costs, three participation scenarios and a break-even figure.

Example only: 25 collected R50 pledges = R1,250. Subtract an estimated R100 setup cost and the model shows R1,150 remaining. Those aren’t actual proceeds, and extra costs reduce the result.

The principal chooses an idea. Planning and approval happen before it becomes a public event.

That’s the part of development I enjoy: helping someone make a clearer decision.

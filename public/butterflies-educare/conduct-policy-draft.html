Kindness, Respect & Positive Behaviour

At Butterflies Educare, every child deserves to feel safe, welcomed and respected. We guide children to manage their feelings, use gentle hands and resolve difficulties with care. Our approach is supportive and age-appropriate, with the wellbeing of all children as the priority.

Our everyday expectations

  1. We speak kindly and keep our hands and feet to ourselves.
  2. We do not tease, threaten, bully, hit, kick, push or deliberately hurt others.
  3. We include classmates, respect individual differences and ask an adult for help when something is wrong.
  4. We listen to teachers and care for our classroom, learning materials and one another.
  5. We learn from mistakes, make amends where appropriate and practise safer choices.

When there is ongoing unkindness, bullying or fighting

  1. Keep everyone safe. Staff calmly intervene, separate children if needed and provide reassurance and appropriate first aid or support.
  2. Listen and understand. Staff speak to the children involved, consider their ages and needs, and document serious or repeated concerns without labelling a child.
  3. Work with families. Parents or guardians are informed sensitively and invited to discuss what happened and how we can support positive behaviour.
  4. Agree on a support plan. Staff may use closer supervision, clear boundaries, restorative conversations, check-ins and consistent encouragement.
  5. Review together. Where behaviour continues, the principal meets with the family, reviews progress and considers appropriate next steps in the best interests and safety of all children. Any decision about continued attendance is made only after careful review and in line with the school's approved procedures.

Reporting and confidentiality

Parents may raise concerns with a teacher or the principal. The school aims to respond promptly, listen fairly to everyone involved and share only information necessary to address a concern. Children should not be publicly named, shamed or punished in a way that harms their dignity.

Important: This is proposed wording for the principal to approve before it becomes an official school policy. It supplements, and does not replace, the existing Code of Conduct and any applicable safeguarding obligations.
